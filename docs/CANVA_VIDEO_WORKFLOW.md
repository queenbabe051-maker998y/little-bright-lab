# Canva-First Video Workflow — The Little Bright Lab

## Goal
Create the first polished *Animal Sounds Safari* episode using Canva in a browser, with GitHub as the source of truth for the script, prompts, character continuity, and production checklist.

**Important:** Canva is the editing/assembly workspace in this workflow. It is not a guarantee of theatrical-quality 3D character animation. Use original character artwork and animate each shot deliberately; do not substitute a slideshow of unrelated stock elements for the established characters.

## Production steps
1. **Create a 16:9 video design.** Choose a blank video canvas at 1920×1080 if available. Use a 16:9 landscape master for the 60-second episode. Make a separate 9:16 cut only after the main episode is approved.
2. **Prepare the character references.** Keep one approved reference image for each original character: Pip (fluffy orange-and-white puppy, blue collar and gold paw charm), Mimi (pink kitten, green eyes and purple collar), Moomo (black-and-white baby cow, blue collar and gold bell), Dott (yellow duckling with orange beak and feet), and Rumble (golden lion cub with fluffy orange mane). The character sheet should be the source of truth; do not redesign a character from scene to scene.
3. **Build five scene sections from the storyboard.** Keep the timing in the manifest: 10s, 12s, 13s, 15s, and 10s = 60 seconds. Use a distinct background and camera framing for each scene, but reuse the same character references.
4. **Prefer character-led shots.** Use close-ups for reactions, medium shots for dialogue, and wide shots for the safari reveal. Animate position, scale, crop, and camera-like movement with restraint; avoid excessive bouncing, random transitions, and static-image-only scenes.
5. **Use original visuals.** Generate or create original characters and backgrounds. Avoid recognizable copyrighted studio characters, branded elements, and unlicensed music. Check the license/source of each Canva asset before use.
6. **Add narration and sound design.** Place narration first; then add clearly separated animal calls, short transition sounds, and quiet cheerful background music. Keep music below dialogue and leave space around the “guess the sound” pause. Use original/appropriately licensed audio and check any music-specific restrictions.
7. **Add readable captions.** Use large high-contrast words for the animal name and sound. Keep captions short for children ages 4–10. Do not rely on sound alone to teach the answer.
8. **Export and review.** Export MP4 at the highest resolution available on the account. Watch the entire video for character consistency, audio clipping, spelling, safe margins, and timing before publishing.
9. **Archive the production.** Save the Canva design link, exported MP4, and any original source assets in the project record. Keep the script and manifest in GitHub. Canva designs are edited in Canva; GitHub does not automatically sync or render a Canva project.

## Canva licensing checklist for monetized YouTube
- Prefer assets marked **Free** and verify each item's source/license in Canva.
- Canva permits many uses of Free and Pro content in finished designs, including online video, but the license category and specific restrictions matter. Branded/Education content, Popular Music, and some other categories can have different rules.
- Do not redistribute Canva stock items as standalone files, and do not imply that stock assets are exclusive or entirely owned by the channel.
- Keep a simple record of the asset name, source/license, and date checked for every music track and important stock asset.
- Review the current official terms before publishing: https://www.canva.com/policies/content-license-agreement/
- Current plan details: https://www.canva.com/pricing/

## Quality gate before calling an episode complete
- [ ] All five characters match the approved reference designs.
- [ ] Animal sounds are clear, correctly paired, and separated from narration.
- [ ] The child has time to guess before the reveal.
- [ ] Music supports the scene without overpowering voices.
- [ ] Captions are readable on a phone and contain no spelling errors.
- [ ] No Pro-only watermark or unlicensed music is present.
- [ ] Exported file plays from start to finish and is the intended duration.
- [ ] Final MP4 is saved separately from the Canva design.

## Scope boundary
This guide prepares the Canva workflow. It does not mean an MP4 has already been generated, nor does GitHub automatically control Canva's editor. The actual footage/design must be created and exported in Canva before it can be treated as a finished episode.
