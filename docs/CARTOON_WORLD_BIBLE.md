# The Little Bright Lab — Cartoon World Bible

Audience: children ages 4–10.

## Creative promise
Joyful, story-led educational animation with expressive original animal characters, cinematic but gentle lighting, rich moving environments, purposeful camera movement, playful reveals, clear narration, and carefully mixed music and sound effects. Aim for premium theatrical family-animation quality without copying any existing studio's specific characters or branding.

## Current character continuity — Animal Sounds Safari
- **Pip:** fluffy orange-and-white puppy; warm brown eyes; floppy ears; blue collar with gold paw-print charm. Energetic, curious, and bouncy.
- **Mimi:** soft pink kitten; green eyes; tiny pink nose; purple collar with heart charm. Gentle, playful, and encouraging.
- **Moomo:** black-and-white baby cow; cream muzzle; large brown eyes; blue collar and small gold bell. Calm and affectionate.
- **Dott:** tiny bright-yellow duckling; orange beak and feet; glossy dark eyes; small rounded wings. Quick, cheerful waddler.
- **Rumble:** friendly golden baby lion; rounded fluffy orange mane; amber eyes. Confident but silly, never frightening.

Keep names, face shapes, markings, eye colors, proportions, accessories, and material treatment consistent from shot to shot. Use the same approved character reference images for every generated scene. Older concept descriptions are superseded by the current definitions above.

## Five educational series
1. **Animal Sounds Safari** — listen, guess, reveal, repeat; animal sounds, animal names, and habitats.
2. **Little Number Adventures** — counting, number recognition, shapes, patterns, and beginner maths.
3. **Alphabet & Word Explorers** — letter recognition, letter sounds, vocabulary, and early reading.
4. **Bright Smiles Club** — brushing teeth, handwashing, bathing, and everyday healthy habits.
5. **Growing Up Great** — kindness, sharing, friendship, confidence, patience, and naming feelings.

## Animation and audio rules
- Use a TV-cartoon rhythm rather than static slides: expressive body acting, blinking, purposeful gestures, secondary motion, moving backgrounds, visual jokes, and motivated camera moves.
- Keep backgrounds colorful but readable, with clear character silhouettes and controlled depth of field.
- One narrator or character speaks at a time. Leave a beat after questions so children can answer.
- Keep music quieter than dialogue and featured animal sounds. Avoid sudden loud effects.
- Build every episode around a simple story and an active child-participation moment.
- Master format may be 16:9 for the main episode; keep key action centered so it can be adapted to 9:16 Shorts.

## Production truth
GitHub Actions can validate assets, run rendering/editing scripts, assemble footage, mix audio, and publish artifacts. It does not create cinematic AI footage without a connected generation service or pre-generated scene clips. A successful workflow run is not proof of studio-grade animation quality.
